01 / Start here

You can understand the viewer in five moves.

Let the story play first. Open the deeper evidence only when a decision makes you curious.

3

Read the right side.

The upper panel tells you what is happening now. The decision rail below tells you what somebody had to decide.

02 / Read the screen

Every part of the screen answers a different question.

The map gives you place. The ribbon gives you time. The panels give you the story and the reason behind each choice.

03 / Read one decision

The water decision shows the whole technique in one example.

On day two, an agent had to plan extra water support before every town had finished reporting its need.

The answer moved from 22, to 24, and back to a valid 22.

  1. Plain written notes proposed 22 trucks.The quantity fit. The answer still failed because some place names were not allowed and required unknowns were missing.
  2. The evidence table made every claim traceable.The answer named its sources and unknowns. It then proposed 24 trucks even though only 22 were available.
  3. Ordinary code named the exact mistake.The checker contained no AI model. It reported that the answer asked for two trucks more than the exercise allowed.
  4. One correction produced a valid plan for 22 trucks.The agent used the same reports. It received no new facts and changed only the part that broke the rule.

The eight small cells carry two readings.

Fill shows whether a try made the same choice as the answer on screen. Color shows whether that try passed every written check.

Filled blueThe try made the shown choice and passed every check.
Outlined blueThe try made a different choice and passed every check.
Filled orangeThe try made the shown choice and broke at least one check.
Outlined orangeThe try made a different choice and broke at least one check.
Dim outlineThis moment is descriptive. It carries no count in the registered result.
04 / Understand the result

The experiment measured rule-following and traceability.

One try was one software agent answering one scored decision once. Each method answered five decisions eight times, for 40 tries per method.

0/40

Plain written notes

No answer passed every check. Most plans fit the resource limit. They failed the written requirements for sources, allowed identifiers, or named unknowns.

17/40

An evidence table

Seventeen answers passed every check. Every claim stood beside its source, and unresolved facts stayed visible instead of becoming guesses.

34/40

Table and one correction

Thirty-four answers passed every check. The one bounded message repaired 17 of the table’s 23 broken answers without breaking an answer that already passed.

There are no mystery points.

A pass required one answer to clear every check written before the run:

  • Use only allowed units and places.
  • Stay inside the resource limit.
  • Use information available by the deadline.
  • Name required unknowns.
  • Use nothing learned later.

The result has a firm boundary.

The checks do not grade the real responders. They do not prove that an agent showed better judgment or would have reached more people. The next experiment must add a recorded outcome model and measure reach.

06 / Present it

A clear classroom walkthrough takes about six minutes.

Tell the human story first. Show the graph technique only after the audience understands the decision it is trying to improve.

0:00

Name the human problem.

Read the opening card. People had to send help while reports were incomplete and still changing.

0:45

Begin the replay.

Point out the real ground, the 72-hour ribbon and the right-side story panel.

1:45

Pause at a decision.

Read who had to decide, what the simulated agent chose and the eight agreement cells.

2:45

Open the evidence.

Click the grade for the exact rule. Open the trace and walk from the public record to the corrected choice.

4:00

Press R for the result.

Tell the water example before reading 0/40, 17/40 and 34/40.

5:30

End on the boundary.

The test measured traceability and rule-following. The next test must measure who the plan would reach.

On 28 July 2026, an earthquake struck Kumamoto and responders had to make decisions while information was incomplete. We replayed eleven of those decision moments and scored five of them as an AI experiment. Plain notes passed every prewritten check in 0 of 40 tries. A table that kept claims beside their sources passed 17 of 40. One exact correction message raised that to 34 of 40. The technique made simulated decisions easier to trace and more likely to follow the rules. It has not yet shown that those decisions would help more people.
07 / Keep the boundary clear

The real record and the simulation share one screen.

Rescue World labels their boundary because a convincing picture can make simulated events feel historical.

These parts come from real public records.

  • The terrain and mapped landslide areas.
  • The earthquake and recorded aftershocks.
  • The road restrictions and designated shelters.
  • The official agency updates and real decision deadlines.

These parts are simulated exercise data.

  • Every AI-generated choice and dispatch.
  • Every count of agreement across agent tries.
  • The damage drawn across the ground.
  • The grades produced by the prewritten exercise checks.
08 / Fix common problems

Most problems have a one-key answer.

A panel is covering the world.

Press Esc. It closes the top surface and returns you to the replay.

A keyboard shortcut does nothing.

Close the art-direction panel with Esc. Text fields inside that tool keep the keys you type.

The camera is somewhere confusing.

Press H for the whole-ground home view. Press P if you want the automatic camera tour.

You want the result immediately.

Press R. The result page gives the complete scenario, the concrete water example, every count, the limitation and the next experiment.

You want to inspect every decision.

Press L. Click any row’s trace control to open its evidence walk-through without moving the replay clock.

Another computer cannot open the page.

Both computers must be on the same local network, and the host computer must keep the viewer server running. Use the host computer's LAN address with port 5184, as explained in README.md.
