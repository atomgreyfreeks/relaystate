01The through-lineFrom growth intelligence to timely follow-through

We studied how living systems find what matters—and used that lesson to help AI remember the right decision at the right time.

Living systems extend attention toward pressure, uncertainty and useful signals. We translated that behavior into AI teamwork: keep possible explanations visible, attach evidence to each idea and revisit the places where a decision can still change.

We applied those rules to a seventy-two-hour earthquake exercise. Every action that passed the rules changed which resources and choices remained available later. The emergency moved through collapsed buildings, missing status reports, shelter pressure, water shortages, aftershock safety and a final change in mission.

The simulation revealed the central requirement: when an AI decision creates work for later, the system should make a clear action card and deliver it when that next step becomes possible.

The reported effects are modeled task units inside one emergency exercise.

The finding

Important decisions should return when it is time to act on them.

The action card tells the next decision-making AI what was decided, which resource was assigned, where it went, which reports support it, what remains unanswered and what must happen now.

Rescue World makes that chain visible to a human reader.

Sources: 32 complete simulation runs and the checked action-card experiment.

02How we got hereGrowth intelligence as a rule for organizing AI work

Our earlier tests turned biological growth principles into a practical way to organize AI work.

Roots, fungi and slime moulds sense local conditions and grow toward useful signals. We used the same basic idea to direct AI attention toward uncertain findings, changing evidence and decisions that are becoming ready for action.

Keep possibilities visible

The system keeps possible explanations visible long enough for new evidence to confirm, change or close them.

Carry evidence with claims

Every finding travels with its source, status, place, quantity and unanswered questions.

Reopen one broken step

A precise rule message gives the decision-making AI one chance to repair the exact problem.

Benchmark evidence

AI roles combined conflicting reports into one checked recommendation. The workflow scored 96.52 out of 100 and led the final AI to the required place in all 120 test cases.

In a later earthquake exercise, 34 of 40 decisions passed every rule after the AI was shown the exact rule it had broken and tried once more. These results gave us a tested structure for the continuous simulation.

04The decisive handoffWhere follow-through broke

The system failed to act on an earlier crew assignment when confirmation became due.

At 20:00, the AI had to place response groups across two collapsed buildings. The paired decision then asked it to confirm a group already assigned to the paper mill.

What the full simulation revealed
32complete runs

The paper-mill confirmation stayed unresolved in all 32 complete runs. We then isolated eight saved histories in which the earlier paper-mill assignment was valid. Those eight histories let us test whether the later AI could use that exact assignment.

The research question became precise: how can an important earlier decision return at the exact moment it needs follow-through?
05The mechanismA timed action card

We turned the earlier assignment into a clear piece of work that had to be completed.

Most AI teamwork systems pass notes or summaries forward. During a long task, the next AI usually receives a large history and must work out which earlier detail matters now.

Our system makes an action card whenever a decision creates future work. It schedules the card and places it directly in the next decision-making AI's work when the follow-up becomes possible.

01Decision: which earlier action created the responsibility.
02Commitment: exact resource, destination and quantity.
03Evidence: the public observations supporting the action.
04Unknowns: the questions that remain open.
05Next step: the decision-making AI must confirm or decline.
Required answer

The decision-making AI must answer every action card: confirm or decline.

A confirmation binds the exact assignment. A decline keeps the responsibility visible and records the reason.

Software safety check

Fixed rules verify the evidence, resource, place, quantity and timing.

The simulation changes only after the complete check passes. The recorded AI inputs and replies let another verifier rebuild the result.

07The toolRescue World and MMM

The plain-English finding leads the product. The 3D world and graphs help a person inspect the decision chain.

The intended reading order

A viewer should first understand the situation and the action in ordinary language:

  • Situation: what changed and what was still unknown.
  • Proposal: who or what, how many, and where.
  • Rule check: whether the simulation accepted it, and the exact reason.
  • Effect: which modeled needs changed, stayed open, or were wasted.

The 3D environment supplies place and time. The impact paths show what changed. The expandable AI network shows how the proposal was produced. The action card shows the earlier decision returning when follow-through becomes possible.

Model–Move–Mesh gives the reader three questions.

  • Model: What did the AI believe from the reports available then?
  • Move: What exact proposal entered the modeled run?
  • Mesh: Did the supporting reports, unanswered questions and earlier assignments reach this decision intact?

The public response remains a separate contextual record. The human-readable operational story leads the view.

08The contributionWhat this establishes and where it goes next

Rescue World shows how one decision becomes the next action.

The project connects growth-intelligence principles, evidence-aware AI teamwork, a continuous emergency simulation, a working action-card mechanism and a human-readable inspection tool.

Evidence established so far
  • One continuous earthquake exercise with 414 events, eleven decisions and 32 complete runs.
  • One exact follow-up that remained unresolved across all 32 complete runs.
  • Eight saved histories in which Qwen3-32B used the action card to complete the exact follow-up on its first attempt.
  • Eight more exact first-attempt confirmations from Qwen3.5-122B.
  • Zero false claims that an unsupported task was complete.
Evidence boundary
  • Claims cover modeled decisions and modeled task units inside this exercise.
  • The public record supplies historical context for the simulation.
  • Real-world operational effectiveness requires separate field evidence.
  • General reliability requires additional incidents and model families.
Next research step

Apply the action card across a second incident and unrelated model families, then study how people and AI can create, route and close these cards during changing operations.

The finding is simple: when an AI makes a decision that will matter later, the system should create a clear action card and deliver it to the person or AI handling the next step exactly when they need it.
