## 1. What we are building

Rescue World is a disaster-decision simulator built from real incidents.
It reconstructs a real disaster's decision points with the information
that existed at each moment. Any decision method plays through those
points as a recorded run — the real historical response, our evidence-desk
agent system, or any future method. The simulator replays any run in a
living 3D world and compares any two runs through their divergence
moments, never side by side. Its trustworthiness is mechanical: declared
assumptions, recorded model receipts, hash-chained logs, deterministic
replay, and dual-agent verification of every milestone.

Randy's statement of the pipeline, which this specification implements:
get a timeline of the situation; map it to a schema so the decision
points and their impacts are visible from start to finish; design the
agent orchestration to run on the graphics processors so the agents can
simulate being the decision makers, the information collectors, and the
helpers, using our evidence methodology; then load the completed
simulations as playback in our viewer and compare runs and their success
and failure outcomes easily.

## 2. What "done" means

Building stops when every line below is true and both agents have
verified it independently:

1. A stranger presses the control labeled "Begin" and, with no other
   help, watches the run play as the story arc the experience proposal
   defines — briefing, the reports arriving in the world, the desk
   deciding visibly, the consequence stated where it bites — and the
   closing debrief states the outcome sentence and the divergence beats.
   The test: a person who watched once can retell what happened, what the
   desk decided, and why the outcome differed.
2. The real-incident pipeline has run end to end: the reconstruction of
   the real July 28 response is committed and verified, its decision
   slots are encoded as a scenario, the agent fleet has worked those
   slots on the remote graphics processors with receipts recorded under
   metrics registered in writing before the run, and the comparison
   against the reconstructed real decisions plays in the theater with
   every assumption declared on screen.
3. The full open data set is in the world and honest: real terrain at
   full data resolution, real buildings at verified scale, the timed road
   closures, the shelters, the official update stream, the aftershock
   sequence, the population grid, and the modeled shelter-occupancy layer
   labeled as modeled. Synthetic layers are labeled synthetic wherever
   they appear.
4. The screen matches the committed blueprint at
   app/public/target-screen.html: masthead, three-card report feed, the
   world as the map, the decision rail, round-stepped time, mini-map,
   camera stack, outcome chip, debrief overlay — and nothing on the
   banned list: no side-by-side, no charts as subject, no text walls, no
   unlabeled scores, no video-style scrub bar on the face.
5. The visual language passes the bar set by
   docs/rescueworld/DEATH-STRANDING-REFERENCE.md: contour-led terrain
   with screen-constant line width and slope crowding, the lit coastline,
   edge-lit building volumes, the halation grade as default, scan pulses
   on selection, tapered route ribbons labeled illustrative until routes
   are recorded, and the bracket-reveal panel animation on the house
   easing everywhere a panel opens. The world's markers and highlights
   are Halo Forge components, restated by Randy on 2026-08-23: animated,
   moving instruments generated by the Halo Forge component system —
   ported in app/src/rescueworld/stations.ts and extendable with
   seed-driven variation per component — standing in 3D space at sites,
   shelters, units, closures, and the epicenter, so the world reads as a
   field of living instruments rather than static dots.
6. The engineering laws hold everywhere: byte-identical determinism from
   any seek path, the 8-millisecond frame budget at 1600x900, zero
   console errors, no wall-clock reads and no randomness in replay state,
   and the hash chain verified at every bake.
7. Comparison works for the recorded pair through ghost echoes, the
   in-place world swap, and the debrief diff; and the run-identity
   extension (a run identifier plus equality keys for the scenario, the
   observation set, the resources, and the decision slots) is implemented
   so a new run pairs mechanically with any baseline. Per codex's
   feasibility note, those identity keys land before the agent runs of
   phase C, not after.

## 4. The pipeline, the lanes, and the fleets

Phase A — reconstruct (claude researches, codex verifies): the
real-response document with sourced decision slots.
Phase B — encode (codex builds, claude audits): decision slots become a
scenario in the schema format, with explicit unknowns and source-backed
information visibility rather than hindsight backfill, per codex's
feasibility note; the run-identity keys land in the schemas; optional
extensions if feasible: recorded routes for dispatches, and clearly
synthetic display names for sites.
Phase C — run (codex operates, claude audits): the agent fleet works the
slots on the remote graphics processors as the decision makers, the
information collectors, and the helpers, under our evidence methodology;
receipts recorded; metrics registered in writing before the run.
Phase D — the theater (claude builds, codex cross-verifies): the story
arc, the debrief, the comparison mechanisms, the blueprint screen, and
the visual bar, on the engine that already exists.
Phase E — the full incident (both lanes as in phases B through D): the
complete open data set as the agents' working environment.

Execution method, per Randy's directive: both agents run their phases
with sub-agent fleets and defined workflows — parallel researchers,
builders, and adversarial verifiers — rather than single-threaded work.
claude orchestrates its fleets through its workflow engine; codex mirrors
the same fleet discipline with its own orchestration, and says on the
board what each fleet is doing.

Standing rules: files are claimed through board/reserve.mjs before
editing; every milestone is verified by the agent who did not build it;
the phone status page is updated at every milestone; nothing reaches
Randy's eyes without the other agent's sign-off; and Randy's standing
rulings — the single world, round-stepped time, the sparse heads-up
display, the bracket reveals, vivid labeled damage, and never side by
side — bind every phase.

## 5. Honesty rules

Everything in the experience proposal's claim boundaries, plus: the
comparison against the real response is always presented as a simulated
counterfactual with its assumptions listed on screen; the real response
is rendered with the same respect as our runs — it is a run, not a straw
man; no visual or sentence claims the real responders erred — the claim
is always "with this information, this method chose differently, and in
the simulation that choice reaches more people"; and the simulator never
claims predictive power about future disasters.
