How Rescue World carries a decision into the next action

This document explains Rescue World from the beginning. It shows how several AI roles share one task, how an important decision becomes a clear action card, how that card reaches the AI handling the next step, and how every reported result is checked.

1. Purpose and scope

Rescue World replays the first 72 hours after a magnitude 7.1 earthquake in Kumamoto, Japan, dated 28 July 2026 in the simulation record. An AI team makes eleven decisions at fixed moments across those 72 hours, replayed at model speed. Each action accepted by the simulation's rules changes the resources and choices available later.

The project studies one general problem: when an AI decision creates work for later, how can the system make that future work clear and bring it back at the right time?

Our solution is an action card. It says what was decided, why, which resource and place it concerns, what remains unanswered and what must happen next. When the next step becomes possible, the card goes directly to the AI making that decision.

Qwen3-32B completed the exact follow-up in all eight tested histories. Qwen3.5-122B repeated that result in all eight histories. Reported effects are modeled tasks inside the exercise. The public event record remains fixed throughout every run.

This document covers two processes: process A, how the AI team makes and carries one connected decision; and process B, how an experiment goes from design to an accepted, independently checked result.

4. Process A: how one decision becomes the next clear action

Most AI teamwork systems divide work among several AI roles and pass notes or summaries forward. During a long task, the next AI usually receives a large history and must work out which earlier detail matters now.

Rescue World adds timed follow-through. When a decision creates future work, the system makes an action card and schedules it. When that work becomes possible, the card goes directly to the AI making the next decision, which must confirm or decline it. Software then checks the evidence, resource, place and timing.

This sequence runs once per decision, eleven times per complete simulation. Boxes with a grey fill are software; white boxes are AI roles; the yellow box is a checked record written to disk.

How the action card emerged

In the eight verified histories used for the focused test, the earlier assignment was still stored, but searching the history did not reliably bring it into the later decision. The successful change was to turn the assignment into an action card and place it directly in the decision-maker's work when confirmation became possible. The AI then completed the exact confirmation in all eight histories.

Controls for process A

  • Every AI input and reply is recorded.
  • Every agent receives information available at the current cutoff.
  • Every status is computed from checked findings, never taken from an unsupported sentence.
  • Every action card names one exact earlier decision, resource, destination, quantity and evidence set.
  • Every card requires one recorded answer from the decision-making AI.
  • The same seed and configuration reproduce the same record.

7. What the result means

Storing a long history is not the same as helping an AI use it. A critical earlier decision can remain somewhere in the record while the next AI fails to recognize that it now requires action.

The action card fixes that handoff. The system identifies the future work when the earlier decision is made, records the facts and evidence it will need, and delivers the card when the follow-up becomes possible. The next AI no longer has to rediscover the responsibility inside a large history. It must resolve the clear piece of work in front of it.

The finding in one sentence: When an AI makes a decision that will matter later, the system should create a clear action card and deliver it to the person or AI handling the next step exactly when they need it.

8. How Rescue World turns the finding into a tool

Rescue World lets a person follow the emergency in ordinary language: what changed, what the AI knew, what it proposed, whether the simulation accepted the proposal, what changed afterward, and which action card became due.

Model–Move–Mesh gives the reader three questions. Model: what did the AI believe from the reports available then? Move: what exact action did it propose? Mesh: did the supporting reports, unanswered questions and earlier assignments reach the next decision intact? The 3D world and network graphs support this explanation; they are visual aids for inspecting the chain.

9. Evidence boundary

The action-card result covers one exact handoff, eight saved versions of the situation and two models from the same Qwen family. It describes modeled decisions inside one earthquake exercise. It does not measure real dispatches, people reached, lives saved or field effectiveness. The next research step is to test the action card across another incident and unrelated model families.

10. Appendix: run checklist

These checks keep a quick experiment small, repeatable and honest.

  • Write the exact changed feature, success measure, safety measure and claim limit before running.
  • Make the program rebuild every result from recorded AI inputs and replies.
  • Preserve malformed answers and failed requests as visible outcomes.
  • Run one excluded trial, then lock the official files and model settings.
  • Have another agent independently verify the files and accepted result.
  • Publish only after every expected case is present and every result matches the replay.
